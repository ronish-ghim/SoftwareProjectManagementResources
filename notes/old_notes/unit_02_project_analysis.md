## Economic Analysis

Economic analysis project analysis involves evaluating the project by analyzing financial feasibility of a project.
This includes identifying and estimating costs of resources such labor, materials, equipment, forecasting the project's revenue or savings, and assessing (i.e. judging) the potential risks and benefits of the project.
The goal of economic analysis is to determine whether project is financially viable (i.e. feasible or workable) and to provide information that can be used to make decisions about the project's allocation of resources.
There are several bases for comparing the worthiness of the projects.
Some bases are:
*Present Worth Method*
*Future Worth Method*
*Annual Worth Method*
*Rate of return method*
*Internal Rate of Return (IRR) Analysis*
*Cost-Benefit Ratio (CBR) Analysis*

### Present Worth Method

It is also called Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) or Present value (PV) or Net Present Value (NPV).
The NPV represents the difference between the present value of the cash inflows and the present value of cash outflows.
In this method of comparison, the cash flows of each alternative will be reduced to time zero by assuming an interest rate*** i***.
If the NPV is positive, it indicates that the investment is expected to be a good investment and if NPV is negative, it means that the investment is not expected to be a good investment.
Then, depending on the type of decision, the best alternative will be selected by comparing the present worth amounts of the alternatives.
In a*** cost dominated cash flow diagram***, the costs (outflows) will be assigned with positive sign and the profit, revenue, salvage value (all inflows), etc. will be assigned with negative sign.
In a*** revenue/profit-dominated cash flow diagram***, the profit, revenue, salvage value (all inflows to an organization) will be assigned with positive sign and the costs (outflows) will be assigned with negative sign.
Here*** P*** represents an initial investment and Rj the net revenue at the end of the jth year. The interest rate is*** i***, compounded annually.
***S*** is the salvage value at the end of the wth year.

### Future Worth Method

the future worth method comparison alternatives, the future worth of various alternatives will be computed.
Then, the alternative with the maximum future worth of net revenue or with the minimum future worth of net cost will be selected as the best alternative for implementation.
Here* P* represents an initial investment,* Rj* the net-revenue at the end of the* j*th year, and* S* the salvage value at the end of the* n*th year.
To find the future worth of the revenue dominated cash flow diagram for a given interest rate, the formula

$$
\text{FW}(i)=-P+R1\frac{1}{(1+i)^{1}}+R2\frac{1}{(1+i)^{2}}+\dots+Rj/[1/(1+j)j]+Rn+S
$$

Here* P* represents an initial investment,* Rj* the net-revenue at the end of the* j*th year, and* S* the salvage value at the end of the* n*th year.
In the above formula, the expenditure is assigned with negative sign and the revenues are assigned with positive sign.
If we have some more alternatives which are to be compared with this alternative, then the corresponding future worth amounts are computed and compared.
Finally, the alternative with the maximum future worth amount should selected the best alternative.

### Annual Worth Method

Annual Worth means that all incomes and expenditures (irregular and
un-uniform) are converted into equivalent uniform annual amount, which is the same each period AW value of the alternatives are calculated for one life cycle only.
If the project is continued for more than one cycle, the equivalent annual worth for the next cycle and all succeeding cycles will be exactly the same.
It is because the provided cash flows are same for each
cycle in constant-value
The repeatability of the uniform annual series through various life cycles can be demonstrated by considering the
following cash-flow diagram which represents two life cycles of an asset with a first cost of $20,000, an annual
operating cost of $8000, and a 3-year life.
*The AW for first life cycle (i.e., 3 years) would be calculated as *

$$
follows: \text{AW} = -20000 (\text{A}/\text{P}, 22\%, 3) - 8000
$$

$$
= -\$17793 The \text{AW} for second life cycles is calculated as \text{AW} = -20000(\text{A}/\text{P}, 22\%, 6)
$$

$$
-(20000)(\text{P}/\text{F}, 22\%, 3)(\text{A}/\text{P}, 22\%, 6)
$$

*-8000*

$$
= -\$17793
$$

The AW value for the first life cycle is exactly the same as that for two life cycles.
This same AW will be obtained when three, four, or any other number of life cycles are evaluated.
Thus, the AW for one life cycle of an alternative represents the equivalent uniform annual worth of that alternative every time the cycle is repeated.
When an asset has a terminal salvage value, then AW can be calculated as:

$$
\text{AW} = -P(A/P, i, n) + S(A/F, i, n)

### Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

The internal rate of return (IRR) is a discounting cash flow technique which gives a rate of return earned by a project.
The internal rate of return is the discounting rate where the total of initial cash outlay and discounted cash inflows are equal to zero.
"*It is the break even interest rate which equates the present worth of a project's cash outflows to the present worth of cash inflows.*"
***NPW= PW of benefits - PW of costs = 0*** It can be used as financial metric to evaluate the performance of an investment or project.
IRR is commonly used to compare the profitability of different Investments or projects and to determine the expected return on an investment A higher value of it's IRR indicates that an investment or project is more profitable.

### Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR)

Benefit-Cost Ratio" refers to the financial metric that helps in assessing the viability of an upcoming project based on its expected costs and benefits.
In other words, the ratio determines the relationship between the expected incremental benefit from a project and the corresponding costs that would be incurred to complete the project.
Benefit-cost ratio (BCR) analysis is a method used determine the economic feasibility of a project or investment.
It involves comparing the total benefits of a project to its total costs, and expressing the result as a ratio.
A BCR greater than 1 indicates that the project with good investment and BCR less than 1 indicates that the project with poor investment.
***BCR=*** PresentValueofExpectedBenefit PresentValueofExpectedCost

### Comparison of Mutually Exclusive Alternatives

Independent Alternatives When alternatives are mutually exclusive only one can be selected because only one is needed.
Therefore, the alternatives are* compared against* *each other* and the one having the most favorable PW is identified as "*the best*".
For example, evaluating locations for construction of a new manufacturing facility, only one site would be selected because only one is needed.
Independent Alternatives As soon as the best one is identified, all of the others are automatically excluded.
All alternatives which yield a return of at least the minimum acceptable rate of return (MARR) are accepted.
terms present worth analysis, all alternatives which have a PW equal to or greater
than 0 when*** i*** is equal to the MARR would be considered as acceptable alternatives.
Independent Alternatives When comparing mutually exclusive alternatives, BCR is also used to compare the relative feasibility of each alternative.
This would be done by calculating the BCR for each alternative and comparing the results, with the highest BCR is considered most economically feasible.
